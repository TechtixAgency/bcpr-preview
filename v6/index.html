Reading an ear tattoo

Three parts, one clinic.

BC tattoos follow one format: the animal's number, the clinic's two- or three-letter code, then a year letter. The lookup decodes it and tells you whether the clinic is still open, or which clinic holds its records now.

Year letters repeat every 21 years, so L, M, N, P and R each show two possible years. The guide has no phone numbers; call the clinic named, or call us.

Licence or registry?

Different things. Many pets need both.

People mix these up all the time, and it leads to the wrong expectations about fees. Here's the one-minute version.

A city licence

Municipal. Required for dogs in most BC cities, and for cats in some. You buy it from your city, usually every year, and it comes with a tag.

It helps your city's animal control return your pet locally and funds their services.

Check your city's licence page

BC Pet Registry

Provincial. The pet ID database behind your pet's microchip or tattoo. It's searchable by shelters and vets across BC and, through AAHA, North America.

Registration is included with BC Pet Registry chips, and the record lasts for life.

Check if you need to register

Home / Travelling to the US

Taking your dog to the US?

Since August 1, 2024, the US CDC has required every dog entering the United States to meet four conditions. Here they are in plain language, plus what the registry can do for you.

Rules can change. Check cdc.gov before you travel. Cats are not covered by this rule.

Photo: dog in the back seat, road trip
Section · 2-col: Steps | Card
The CDC's four requirements

Every dog entering the US must…

Be at least 6 months old

On the day of entry. No exceptions for puppies.

Have an ISO-compatible microchip

A 15-digit chip, implanted before any rabies vaccination the paperwork relies on. Every BC Pet Registry chip qualifies.

Have a CDC Dog Import Form receipt

Filled in online before you travel. It's free and the receipt is valid for six months of entries.

Appear healthy on arrival

Extra rules apply if the dog has been in a high-risk rabies country in the last six months. Canada is not one.

Who pays the $45 registration fee?

Only owners registering a chip that didn't come from BC Pet Registry. Chips your vet, shelter or the BC SPCA buys from us include lifetime registration, so most people pay nothing.

How much does it cost to microchip a dog or cat in BC?

Typically $25–$120, depending on the clinic. Registration is included with a BC Pet Registry chip; otherwise $45 for life. Full cost guide. Our community microchip clinics are free, including registration.

Does my clinic pay a fee to register chips?

Not for BC Pet Registry chips. Registration is built into the chip, so there is nothing to pay for your clinic or your client. If a client brings a chip from another supplier, the owner registers it themselves for a one-time $45.

I adopted from the BC SPCA. Do I need to register?

No. Your pet was registered on adoption day. Activate your account with the email you gave the shelter, then check your details.

Is a city licence the same as registering with BC Pet Registry?

No. A licence is a municipal requirement you buy from your city each year. BC Pet Registry is the provincial pet ID database behind your pet's microchip or tattoo. Many pets need both.

Which animals can be registered?

Dogs, cats, rabbits, ferrets, birds and horses. (Final list to be confirmed.)

How do I transfer ownership?

Sign in and use the transfer tool. Transfers are free. Shelters transfer at adoption automatically.

Can I see the owner's details if I search a chip?

No. For privacy, the search tells you whether the chip is registered with us and who to call. We contact the owner.

What do the letters in an ear tattoo mean?

Animal number, then a two- or three-letter clinic code, then a year letter. Decode a tattoo or download the code guide.

What does my dog need to enter the US?

To be at least 6 months old, have an ISO microchip, a CDC Dog Import Form receipt and appear healthy. Full checklist.

How do shelters get non-profit chip pricing?

Email info@bcpetregistry.ca with your organisation's details. Pricing is applied to your store account.

How much does it cost to microchip a dog or cat in BC?

Microchipping costs $25 to $120 at a BC veterinary clinic, depending on the clinic. Registration with BC Pet Registry is included when the clinic uses a BC Pet Registry chip; otherwise it's a one-time $45 for life. Community microchip clinics are free.

Microchipping

Any other chip

Registration

$45 for life

One payment, no renewals, for chips from any other supplier or registry. Transfers and updates are free afterwards.

What the clinic's price covers

The chip is a glass capsule the size of a grain of rice, implanted under the skin with a needle in a few seconds. The clinic's fee covers the chip, the implant and the appointment. Some clinics include it in a vaccination or spay/neuter visit, which is why prices vary so much.

Two things are not in that price: a city licence, which your municipality sells separately, and, for chips from other suppliers, registration with BC Pet Registry.

Why "is it registered?" matters more than the price

A chip carries a number and nothing else. It only brings a pet home if that number is linked to a phone that rings. Registry chips sold to participating vets, shelters and the BC SPCA come with lifetime registration built in. If your pet's chip came from somewhere else, it isn't in our database until you add it.

Where did your pet's microchip come from?

Three questions people ask next

Is microchipping cheaper for cats than dogs?

Usually the same. The chip and the procedure are identical; price differences come from the clinic, not the species.

Does the $45 ever apply to a BC Pet Registry chip?

No. Registration is built into the chip's price, so neither the clinic nor the owner pays it. If you were asked to pay for a registry chip, call us.

Is there a cheaper way to get a chip?

Yes. Our community microchip clinics, funded by registry revenue and grants, chip and register pets for free. See upcoming clinics.

Prices are what BC clinics typically charge; confirm with yours. Registry fees are set by BC Pet Registry and shown on every page of this site.

Rhythm

Cloud → white → cloud → blue-green band → white → cloud → hummingbird block → cloud footer with an SPCA-blue bar: the same surfaces, in the same proportions, as spca.bc.ca. Never two of the same surface in a row.

One scroll-driven set piece per page. Everything else reveals on enter or lifts on hover.

Motion and access

  • Headings, cards and the proof photo move with the scroll (scrubbed), never on a timer; parallax capped at 40px
  • Reduced motion: no parallax, no crossfades, steps as a plain list
  • Below 900px the stage becomes a sticky strip; the tabs scroll sideways
  • Every interactive element works with a keyboard
Compare with V7 "You first": same system, the hero asks "who are you?" instead and the check comes second, with the five steps as a pinned horizontal track. Test both homepages with the first-click tasks in the UX brief.
